01

Our commitment

This website is designed and built to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA. That is our target, not a certification: the site has not yet had a formal, independent accessibility audit, and we will update this statement when it has.

02

What we have built in

  • A “skip to content” link, a consistent header and footer, and headings in a logical order.
  • Pages, menus and forms are designed to work with a keyboard alone, with a visible focus outline.
  • Text and controls are designed to meet AA color contrast; color is never the only way information is shown.
  • Images and maps have text alternatives, and each map sits beside a written summary of what it shows.
  • Maps open at full size in a new view, and each map has a text summary alongside it.
  • Forms have visible labels, clear required fields and error messages, and a confirmation announced to screen readers.
  • Pages reflow to a single column on small screens and support browser zoom to 200% and beyond.
03

Known limitations

  • Detailed maps. Map images contain fine labels and legends. We provide written summaries and full-size downloads, but some map detail may be hard to read with low vision or a screen reader.
  • Third-party documents. Some County and agency PDFs we link to are scanned or not tagged for accessibility. We cannot change them, but we will help you get the information they contain.
  • Petition signature. An accessible signature method must be confirmed before online petition signing is enabled.
04

Report a barrier

If something on this site is hard or impossible to use, please tell us. It helps to include:

  1. The page, map or form, or its web address.
  2. What you were trying to do, and what got in the way.
  3. The device, browser and any assistive technology you use, if you are comfortable sharing it.
  4. How you would like us to reply, or the format you need the information in.

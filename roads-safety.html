01 Street Lighting

Street lighting should respond to public safety needs.

Does someone have to get hurt first? The Community Plan takes a restrictive approach to street lighting and allows lighting when public safety is at risk.

Why it matters

Limited street lighting can make it more difficult and dangerous to travel at night, increasing the risk of crashes and affecting the safety of residents, students, commuters and emergency responders. It directly impacts the health, safety and welfare of our community.

Community Plan rule

“Street lighting may be considered when public safety is at risk.”

Actual safety needs and FHWA guidance

Actual safety needs

Adequate street lighting is important for:

  • Visibility for drivers
  • Intersections and high-risk areas
  • Pedestrians and cyclists
  • Night travel and daily commutes

Research

FHWA explains that modern lighting can precisely target where light is needed while minimizing spill into the night sky and neighboring properties. Rural character should drive better design, not end the safety discussion.

02 Gunn Highway

Expansion of Gunn Highway is restricted.

The Community Plan identifies Gunn Highway as a road with restrictions on further expansion; the County’s constrained-road table also references the Community Plan for the section from South Mobley Road to Pasco County.

Why it matters

Limits on expanding Gunn Highway affect traffic, safety and access for residents, commuters, students and emergency services.

19,912Daily vehicle movements. Two lanes.

The County’s 2024 data put Gunn Highway from the Pasco County line to Van Dyke Road at 19,912 annual-average daily vehicle movements. Both daily and peak-direction service are rated E against a D standard.

These figures demand a serious response.

03 Walking, Biking & Crossings

Pedestrians and cyclists face safety risks.

The safety of pedestrians, cyclists, and people crossing the road needs to be considered together with traffic, speed, crossings, and the actual conditions on each corridor.

Why it matters

Limited and unsafe conditions for pedestrians and cyclists increase the risk of crashes and affect the safety of residents, students, commuters and everyone who uses these roads. Safe walking and biking routes are essential for a safer, healthier and more connected community.

Community Plan rule

Includes pedestrian/equestrian crossings, traffic controls, trails and bikeways.

Actual conditions and County policy

Actual conditions

  • Traffic and higher speeds
  • Limited pedestrian and bicycle facilities
  • Safety at intersections and crossings
  • Continuous routes are limited

Safe walking should not be an exception.

County Mobility Objective 2.2 calls for protecting pedestrians, children, seniors and people with disabilities. FHWA recommends connected walking routes without gaps. A safe route matters whether you are going to school, visiting a neighbor or using a wheelchair.

04 Emergency Access

Emergency access must be reliable.

The road network serves ambulances, fire services, police, and emergency travel. Access and travel time need to be considered in decisions about road infrastructure.

Why it matters

Reliable emergency access is essential for residents, students, commuters and the entire community. Delays can affect emergency response times and public safety. Road decisions should support faster, safer emergency access for the community.

Community Plan rule

The plan includes policies related to emergency access, traffic controls and roadway improvements.

The Community Plan includes content about emergency access as part of transportation and roadway policies.

Key factors

  • Emergency response time
  • Intersections and road design
  • Congestion and delays
  • Road conditions
  • Access during severe weather

Two planning frameworks. Two approaches to roads and safety.

Both plans address roads and safety, but they take different approaches. The Community Plan sets area-specific rules for Keystone–Odessa, while the Comprehensive Plan provides countywide policies.

Keystone–Odessa Community Plan

Focuses on area-specific rules.

  1. Rural road characteristics

    Should a rural appearance come before people’s daily needs? The plan prioritizes two-lane rural roads and limits changes that increase capacity or urbanize the area.

  2. Road configuration

    Why limit the options before assessing what people need? The plan identifies preferred road configurations, including two-lane rural sections, and the types of improvements considered appropriate for the area.

  3. Gunn Highway

    How much traffic is enough to reconsider the restrictions? Gunn Highway is identified as a road with restrictions on further expansion and included in the constrained-road tables.

  4. Lighting

    Does someone have to get hurt first? The plan allows lighting when public safety is at risk. Identify the danger. Act before someone is injured.

  5. Identified types of transportation improvements

    Should a safety improvement have to pass a rural-character test? The plan allows intersection, cycling, walking and traffic-control improvements while requiring rural character to be maintained.

↔

Hillsborough County Comprehensive Plan

Focuses on related countywide policies.

  1. Transportation safety

    Mobility Element

    Includes countywide policies to improve transportation safety, reduce crashes, and address safety for all users, including drivers, pedestrians and cyclists.

  2. Mobility

    Mobility Element

    Supports a connected and functional multimodal network, including roadways, transit, bicycle and pedestrian facilities.

  3. Infrastructure needs

    Mobility Element

    Addresses transportation infrastructure needs based on current and future conditions, including roadway capacity, intersection improvements and system performance.

  4. Evaluation of conditions

    Mobility Element

    Uses data, level-of-service standards and network evaluation to identify needed improvements across the county.

  5. Planning tools

    Mobility Element

    Provides broader transportation planning tools, such as the Cost Feasible Plan, the Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) and coordination with the Long Range Transportation Plan (LRTP).

A road restriction is not a permanent answer.

County Mobility Policy 5.1.4 calls for the constrained-road list to be reevaluated before each long-range transportation plan update, balancing community preservation, safety and growing traffic congestion. Put Gunn Highway’s restriction back under serious public scrutiny.
